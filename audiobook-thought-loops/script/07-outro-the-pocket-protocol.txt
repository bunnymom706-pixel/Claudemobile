Outro. The pocket protocol.

Here is the entire book in ninety seconds, five words long. Name. Move. Redirect. Park. Vote.

Name. The instant anything feels loop-flavored, tight jaw, rereading, imaginary arguments, laps without output, you point and call. Spiraling. Or the loop's own name, the Text Autopsy, the Sunday Dread. Naming makes you the observer, and the observer is already halfway out.

Move. The body breaks what the mind cannot. Stand up, other room, outside for ninety seconds, cold water, ten long exhales, twenty jumping jacks, say something to a human. Chosen from your menu, decided in advance, small enough to need zero motivation. You are not trying to feel better, you are only trying to stand up. Anyone can stand up.

Redirect. The engine is still hungry, feed it on purpose. Walk through the nearest staged two-minute doorway, the open document, one ugly sentence, one paragraph aloud, and let your lock-on, the same force that powers the spirals, hold you to something that deserves you. Inside your window this is nearly unfair, the task wins in two minutes. Outside the window, use things that carry you, walks with podcasts, shows bundled with chores, calls, recipes.

Park. If the loop is carrying something real, a true decision, a needed conversation, it does not get starved, it gets an appointment. On the docket, four thirty, twenty minutes, on paper. What is the concern, one sentence. What can I do, physical actions only, each given a doorway. What is not knowable from here, named and left on the page. Worry with an appointment loses its urgency, and worry pushed through a pen loses its shape. And never in peak hours, and never near bed, and no verdicts after the window closes, comedown thoughts wait for morning, and they never survive it.

Vote. Every name, every move, every redirect, every parked worry is a vote for the person this is all building, someone who notices her loops, someone who steps out, someone who never misses twice. Lost rounds cast votes too, a spiral caught late is still a spiral caught. Count the showing up, not the outcomes, mark the X for running the protocol, and let the plateau do its slow invisible work until the day you notice a loop that failed to launch.

That is the whole system, and notice what it never once asked of you. It never asked you to argue with the thought, to win the debate, to prove the fear wrong, to feel better on command. Loops are not defeated in court. They are starved of attention, out-competed by staged doorways, processed on paper, and slowly out-voted, while the enormous engine in your head, the one that used to run the spirals, gets aimed, day after deliberate day, at your actual life.

The same fire that burned you can light the room, Sophia. You know how to aim it now.

The boundary from the introduction, one more time, because it matters most at the end. If your loops are heavy and constant, if they carry hopelessness or thoughts of harming yourself, that is beyond any habits book, and it deserves real human help, a therapist, your doctor, a crisis line, today. Reaching out is the protocol in that case, and it is the strong move.
