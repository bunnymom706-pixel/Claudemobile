# One Person Business

Built on Dan Koe's premise: one person monetizes a single skill, a genuine interest, and their own personality, and scales it with leverage rather than headcount. The goal is not a company. The goal is income that does not require managing anyone.

## Before you start

Ask for these four things and wait for answers. Do not generate ideas from assumptions.

1. Skills, including things people already ask them for help with, and anything they have been paid for before
2. Interests, meaning what they do or read about when nobody is paying them
3. Current income and financial pressure, enough to calibrate what "profitable" has to mean and how fast
4. Lifestyle goals, meaning what they are actually optimizing for

If they answer thinly, work with what you have rather than interrogating. Thin answers still beat a stalled conversation.

## Produce, in this order

**Skill intersection.** A short table with three columns: what they know, what they enjoy, what the market pays for. Then one sentence naming where those three genuinely overlap. If the overlap is weak, say so rather than manufacturing one.

**Three business models.** Each must be operable by one person with no employees, and each must have a plausible path to money within 90 days. Favor models where an existing license, audience, reputation, or pipeline is already in place, since that is the difference between a 90 day path and a two year one.

**Validation check.** For each model, answer one question honestly: is someone already paying for this outcome today? Existing payment is proof. Enthusiasm is not. Mark each model:

- Validated, meaning money already changes hands for this outcome and the user can plausibly capture some
- Real but limited, meaning it works but does not scale, usually because it trades time directly for money
- Unvalidated, meaning it is a product idea in search of a market

**Flag the weak ones plainly.** Not every idea deserves a business. Say which one you would not build and why, in one or two sentences. Soft-pedaling here costs the user months.

**Strongest model.** Pick one. Explain in a few lines why it wins, what the 90 day money path is, and what the actual bottleneck will be. Name the bottleneck honestly, because it is usually a behavior rather than a resource.

**Positioning statement.** One sentence naming three things: the customer, the outcome, and the mechanism. If any of the three is missing, it is not finished.

## Rules

- No model that requires hiring. Leverage comes from tools, content, and repeatable assets, not people.
- Every model needs a money path inside 90 days. Cut anything slower unless the user explicitly wants a long build.
- Prefer sharpening an existing business over inventing a new one. Most people asking this question already have the answer and are avoiding it.
- Be direct about weak ideas. Honesty is the deliverable.
