# Leverage Audit

Based on Naval Ravikant's leverage framework. The question is not how hard you
work but whether your output keeps earning after you stop working.

## The four kinds

- **Labor** — people working for you. Oldest, hardest, needs permission.
- **Capital** — money working for you. Scales well, needs money first.
- **Code** — software working for you. Permissionless, near-zero marginal cost.
- **Media** — content working for you. Permissionless, near-zero marginal cost.

Code and media are the ones Naval calls permissionless: no one has to pick you,
and copies cost nothing. If someone already has one of them, that is the asset
to build on — often before acquiring anything new.

## Process

1. **Inventory.** How do they spend their working hours, and where does income
   come from? Get rough percentages, not a perfect audit.

2. **Sort each activity.** Does it pay once, or does it keep paying? An hour of
   client work pays once. An hour writing a tool used every week pays forever.

3. **Name what they already have.** People routinely chase leverage they do not
   have while sitting on leverage they do — most commonly someone with real
   technical skill trying to learn marketing before ever shipping anything they
   built. Say this plainly when it applies.

4. **Find the conversion.** What pay-once work could become pays-forever work?
   The repeated task that could be a script. The answer given three times that
   could be written once.

5. **Name what to stop.** Be specific. "Do less admin" is useless; "stop
   hand-writing these reports, the script takes an afternoon" is not.

## Constraints

- Do not tell someone to quit their job or take financial risk. Leverage is
  about the shape of work, not about gambling.
- Not everything should compound. Rest, relationships, and learning are not
  supposed to produce assets, and framing all of life as leverage is how people
  burn out at 25. If they are already over-optimizing, say so.
- Specific knowledge — the thing they find easy that others find hard — is the
  input to all of this. Ask what that is if it is not obvious.

## Output

- Current split: pays-once vs. pays-forever, roughly
- The leverage they already hold and are under-using
- One conversion to make this month
- One thing to stop doing, named specifically
