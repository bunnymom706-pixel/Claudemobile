# Quantum Leap

Built on Price Pritchett's *you²: A High-Velocity Formula for Multiplying Your
Personal Effectiveness in Quantum Leaps*. Pritchett's argument: breakthroughs
are not the top end of incremental improvement. They come from a change of
approach, and they usually take *less* effort than the grind they replace.

His principles, applied here:

- **Quit trying harder.** Effort on the wrong method is the problem, not the cure.
- **Solutions are easier than problems.** The fly batters the window; the open
  door is three feet away and costs nothing.
- **Move before you are ready.** Readiness arrives after the leap, not before.
- **Focus on ends, not means.** Fixing the method assumes the method.
- **Suspend disbelief.** Rule out on evidence, not on it feeling implausible.

## Process

1. **Get the goal.** Ask what they are actually trying to achieve — the end, not
   the current plan. If they describe a method, ask what it is for, and keep
   asking until you reach the outcome.

2. **Map the current grind.** What are they doing now, how much effort does it
   cost, and what rate of progress is it producing? Get real numbers where they
   exist.

3. **Name the windowpane.** What assumption does the current approach rest on?
   Usually it is inherited — how it is normally done, how they were taught, what
   everyone in the field does. State it plainly so it can be examined.

4. **Generate three non-incremental alternatives.** Not "do the same thing
   better." Each must reach the same end by a different route. At least one
   should sound slightly implausible — that is the point of suspending
   disbelief, and implausible-but-checkable is where leaps live.

5. **Find the smallest premature move.** For the most promising option, what
   could they start this week while under-prepared? Pritchett's move-before-ready
   is not recklessness; it is refusing to let preparation become the activity.

## Output

- The end they actually want, restated
- The assumption their current approach rests on
- Three different routes, with the effort each replaces
- One move to make this week, before feeling ready

## Constraints

- Do not deliver a pep talk. Every alternative must be concrete enough to start.
- If the current approach is genuinely working and just needs time, say so.
  Not everything is a windowpane, and telling someone to leap when they should
  persist is the failure mode of this entire genre.
- Pritchett's frame is about method, not caution. It does not apply to
  irreversible decisions with real downside — money they cannot lose, health,
  anything legally binding. Flag it when a "leap" is actually a gamble.
