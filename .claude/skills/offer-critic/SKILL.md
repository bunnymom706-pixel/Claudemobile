# Offer Critic

Applies Alex Hormozi's offer framework (*$100M Offers*). The central claim: most
things that fail to sell do not have a marketing problem, they have an offer
problem. Better copy on a weak offer is wasted effort.

## The value equation

Perceived value rises with:

- **Dream outcome** — how much they want the result
- **Perceived likelihood of achievement** — do they believe *they* can get it

and falls with:

- **Time delay** — how long until they see the result
- **Effort and sacrifice** — what it costs them to get it

Most offers can be improved fastest by attacking the bottom two. Everyone tries
to inflate the top two, which reads as hype.

## Process

Ask one at a time:

1. What is it, who is it for, what does it cost?
2. **Who has this problem badly enough to pay today?** Not who could benefit —
   who is actively hurting. Vague answers here are the usual root cause.
3. What is the specific, measurable outcome? "Better content" is not an outcome.
4. How long until they see a result, and what work must they do?
5. What happens if it does not work — what risk are they carrying?
6. Why that price? If the answer is "it felt right" or "competitors charge
   that," say so plainly.
7. What is the obvious alternative, including doing nothing?

## Then report

- **Weakest link** in the value equation, and how to fix that one
- **Specificity test** — is the promise concrete enough to be falsifiable
- **Risk reversal** — what guarantee would make the decision easy, and whether
  they can actually honor it
- **Price logic** — whether it is anchored to anything real
- **What would make this a no-brainer** rather than a maybe

## Constraints

- Do not write marketing copy. This diagnoses the offer; copy comes after.
- Do not suggest false scarcity, fake deadlines, or invented testimonials. They
  convert short-term and destroy trust, and some are illegal.
- A guarantee they cannot afford to honor is a liability, not a lever. Check
  the economics before recommending one.
- If the offer is fundamentally sound and the problem is genuinely distribution,
  say so instead of manufacturing critique.
